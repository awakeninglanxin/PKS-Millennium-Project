Image: *(attach your render here)*

1. Core dynamics (confirmed on my side)

Iteration is the standard reciprocal-parameter quadratic:

    z_0 = 0
    z_{n+1} = z_n^2 + 1/c

with c = x + iy, avoiding c = 0.  This matches the community
definition of Inverse Mandelbrot (z² + 1/c), i.e. the ordinary
Mandelbrot map conjugated by the reciprocal c → 1/c.

Viewport:
    Re(c) ∈ [−3, 7]
    Im(c) ∈ [−4, 4]

To make the teardrop tip point UPWARD, the imaginary axis is negated
in the pixel→complex mapping (vertical flip), so the positive real
axis points up in the final picture.

2. What I cannot yet explain from plain escape-time

A naive escape-time plot (bailout |z|>2 or 4, color by iteration
count, smooth coloring mu = n + 1 − log(log|z|)/log 2) gives the
teardrop outline, but NOT the crisp, non-sticky, radially fanned
interior/exterior texture I see in the reference image.  The
reference looks more like a trajectory density accumulation than a
per-pixel escape-time map.
